Nodal

A language built from scratch around strict constraints.

grand — a 5-letter morpheme, all five slots filled.

Every morpheme is 2–5 letters. Every content word begins with a mandatory part-of-speech prefix. No two roots share a meaning. Opposites are always independent roots, never a negated form. And Gliph — the writing system — fits each morpheme into a five-slot cell [A]–[E].

Nodal is not intended to be spoken, adopted, or standardized. It is a finished design artifact — a demonstration that such a language can exist.

Four things to notice

Modular cells [A]–[E]

A morpheme's slot pattern is set by its length. Two-letter morphemes fill [A][E]; three-letter fill [A][C][E]; five-letter fill every slot. Reading order is always A → B → C → D → E, regardless of visual position.

Punctuation is prosody

., !, ?, : are not written as literal glyphs. They render as pause glyphs: bar count sets the level, a modifier sets the type.

Strict constraints

Every morpheme is 2–5 letters — roots, prefixes, suffixes, conjunctions, all of them. No homonyms. Every content word carries a grammatical prefix: no- (noun), ve- (verb), pa- (adjective), po- (adverb/preposition), and so on.

Law of Gradient

Modifiers follow the head word, and the closer they stand, the stronger their accent. no-dom pa-grand-as means “a large house”; pa-grand-as no-dom foregrounds “large” as the topic. Negation (po-ne) is no exception — it is postposed to the word it negates.
